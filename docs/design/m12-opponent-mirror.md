# M12: the other player's mirror (owner's decision, Nov 7, 2026)

In a race on phones ("Play on this phone", 1v1 and the other split formats), each phone shows a small live mirror of the other side, top left, with the clock and both counts beside it. Sketches: [m12-opponent-options.png](m12-opponent-options.png) (A, B, C; the owner picked A) and [m12-opponent-mirror.png](m12-opponent-mirror.png).

The owner's words: "Can we do option A but also show what the remote player is actually doing? Meaning see where their player is, if they're carrying a piece, etc?" and then "Can we not show their tray? Just show the remote player at the top with a relative horizontal position. Show the piece they are holding when they pick it up."

## The rules
1. **Their board only**, no tray: the board's outline, its target shapes faint, and the pieces they've placed filled in, live.
2. **Their dot, live.** On their board, at its place. Anywhere above the board (their tray row), the dot sits on the mini board's top edge at its **relative horizontal position** across their side.
3. **The piece they hold** is drawn with their dot, turned as they turn it, from pickup to drop or snap.
4. Orange marks the other side (frame, dot, "Them 2/4"); your own count stays as today.
5. Where it goes: top left, about a quarter of the board's size, under the top bar and clear of the stick; the clock and counts move to its right. A phone too narrow for it keeps today's count only.
6. Not shown: in solo and team games (one board), on a tablet that already draws the whole match, and on the TV.
7. A 2-v-1: the mirror shows the other side's board and every dot on it.

## Where it goes with each pad (owner, Nov 7 evening: "Go with your recommendation")
8. **Clear of the pad.** The mirror never sits under a control. With the D-pad it goes top left, as rule 5 says. With the stick or tilt, whose area reaches that corner, it goes in the gap between the left control and the board, under the clock, at the same size ([m12-mirror-stick.png](m12-mirror-stick.png)). Where neither place is clear, today's count only. The host phone follows the same rule as a joined phone.
