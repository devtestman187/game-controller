Owner, Nov 8, 2026: "For the cat, consider games like Mario Party and Lego Party. Review their party game mechanics and scoring. Look at what aspects people [like] and research it. It may make sense to knock away an already placed piece."

Context: Thumb Party is a family party game on a TV with phones as controllers, for groups that include children around 8. Shape Race: 1 to 4 players on two sides race to carry, turn and drop shapes into a picture's outlines; a dropped piece that fits locks in place. M14 plans points, a party night of 3 or 5 rounds with a scoreboard, end-of-night awards, and the cat: a chaos event that is warned 2 seconds ahead, walks onto one side's board and bats a piece away; players can shoo it. Today's plan says the cat never touches a locked (placed) piece; the owner asks whether it should be able to knock one loose. Our own characters and art only; describe other games' mechanics in your own words.

Questions (answer each with the URL of the page that says so, and its date):
1. Mario Party (recent ones: Super Mario Party, Mario Party Superstars, Super Mario Party Jamboree): how a party plays (board turns, minigames between turns), how stars and coins score, the events that take things away from players (stealing coins or stars, Bowser and Boo spaces, chance time, item effects), how they are warned or shown, and how often they happen.

6. Scoring in these games: points per minigame, bonus stars at the end, double points, ties, and how the scoreboard is shown between rounds.
