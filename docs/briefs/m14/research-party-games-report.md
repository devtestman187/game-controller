# Mario Party & LEGO Party: party mechanics, scoring, and taking things away
Research for Thumb Party M14 (the cat). Compiled Nov 8, 2026. Every bullet carries its URL and the date I could find on the page.
Rules details from Super Mario Wiki / Wikipedia are marked (wiki). Reviewers' opinions are labelled as opinion.
Access notes: DuckDuckGo's HTML endpoint blocked me once (captcha) but DuckDuckGo Lite worked; Reddit served a login wall, so player threads are missing (see Not confirmed). GameSpot and Forbes returned 403 and were read via Wayback captures.

## 1. How a Mario Party plays, and what takes things away

- *Mario Party Superstars* (Oct 29, 2021): 5 remade N64 boards, 100 curated minigames; four characters roll 1–10, items can affect self/others, a randomly chosen minigame is played between every round, stars cost 20 coins from Toadette, most stars wins — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Party_Superstars (game released Oct 29, 2021; page read Nov 8, 2026).
- *Super Mario Party* (Oct 5, 2018): 4 boards, 80 minigames; the star sits in a random spot and costs **10** coins; "the player who has the most stars and coins by the end of the game wins"; minigames award coins by placement; 2v2 "Partner Party" mode — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Party (game Oct 5, 2018).
- *Super Mario Party Jamboree* (Oct 17, 2024; Switch 2 edition Jul 24, 2025): 7 boards, 112 minigames, 10–30 turns, "after each turn has ended, the players play a minigame", most stars wins, plus a "Pro Rules" ruleset that strips out luck — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Party_Jamboree (game Oct 17, 2024).
- Board-side swing events in Jamboree, per IGN: randomly placed hidden blocks that can contain stars, and "two Bonus Stars handed out after the final turn", so "a match is almost never over until the results screen" — https://www.ign.com/articles/super-mario-party-jamboree-review (Oct 15, 2024).
- Jamboree's Jamboree Buddy doubles every action: buy two stars, use Boo twice, but "landing on a Bowser space will have you visit the big guy twice"; buddies last a few turns and can be swiped by anyone who passes you from behind — same IGN review (Oct 15, 2024).
- Boo (the classic steal): on the board, Boo steals coins for free or a star for 50 coins *on the player's behalf*; in Superstars and Jamboree Boos "steal either coins or stars from an opponent, like in the first three entries" — https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo (wiki; read Nov 8, 2026).
- A victim's counterplay existed in Mario Party 2: "if the Boo tries to steal Coins, the opponent can fight it off by pressing the A button quickly", and Boo can take at most 30 coins or one star — same Boo page (wiki).
- Chance Time: the player landing on the space plays a small minigame that "transfers coins and/or Stars between themselves or other players… has the potential to drastically alter the course of a game"; possible outcomes include "1 Star →", "↔ all coins", "↔ all Stars" — https://www.mariowiki.com/Chance_Time (wiki).
- How often: Chance Time Spaces are "among the rarest type of spaces… generally only one to three of them on each game board"; Jamboree adds more of them only during the Homestretch, and they are **removed entirely under Pro Rules** — https://www.mariowiki.com/Chance_Time_Space (wiki).
- Bowser Spaces are "fairly rare, with one to five on each board by default"; in Jamboree (Impostor Bowser) the roulette offers lose 10/15/20/30 coins, lose half your coins, everyone loses 10 coins, lose 1 Star, Bowser Revolution (all coins split evenly), Bowser Shuffle (all positions shuffled) — https://www.mariowiki.com/Bowser_Space (wiki).
- Bowser's mercy rules: if the player has no Stars, in Jamboree he gives 20 coins when they are 3rd or last, and if last he may give 20 coins and skip the event; under Pro Rules the roulette is replaced by "Impostor Bowser always steals a Star" — same Bowser Space page (wiki).
- In-game warnings are the space itself plus a roulette: "all characters have a visibly frightened expression during the roulette" in Jamboree; Superstars shows six options in the first half of a match and 7–8 in the second — same Bowser Space page (wiki).
- Eurogamer notes a non-mechanical warning channel (character reaction emojis one can fire during other players' turns): "character reaction emojis you can deploy during other people's turns to let out cries of 'Betrayal!' and 'Whaaat?'" — https://www.eurogamer.net/super-mario-party-jamboree-review (Oct 15, 2024).
- Hearts of the loop: Nintendo Life played Jamboree with "two young kids (aged 7 and 10)" and calls it "a proper evening staple"; it also notes the option to "give younger players a boosted star start" — https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/super-mario-party-jamboree (Oct 15, 2024).
- Jamboree Pro Rules are locked to 12-turn games, "around 90 minutes a pop" — same Nintendo Life review (Oct 15, 2024).

## 2. LEGO Party! (2025, SMG Studio)

- Credits, correctly: developed by SMG Studio, **published by Fictions** (the new label that took over Private Division's titles), released Sep 30, 2025 on Switch/PS4/PS5/PC/Xbox, Switch 2 version Jul 30, 2026 — https://www.lego.com/en-us/aboutus/news/2025/june/lego-party (Jun 6, 2025) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_Party (wiki, read Nov 8, 2026). Nintendo's store lists Publisher: Fictions, Developer: SMG Studio — https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/lego-party-switch/ (read Nov 8, 2026).
- Shape of a party: 4 Challenge Zones (Ninjago, Pirate, Space, Theme Park), 60 minigames, up to 4 players local or online/crossplay — https://legoparty.com/ (official, read Nov 8, 2026); session length runs "as short as six rounds or roughly 45 minutes… up to three-hour-long, 24-round epics" — https://www.ign.com/articles/lego-party-review (Sep 30, 2025).
- The loop is inverted versus Mario Party: the minigame **opens** each round, its placement pays studs and sets that round's turn order; players vote for the minigame by standing on one of four tiles (three named games, one random) — https://www.superphillipcentral.com/2026/01/lego-party-multi-review.html (Jan 1, 2026); same turn-order change described at https://web.archive.org/web/20260121151752/https://www.gamespot.com/articles/lego-party-fixes-mario-partys-biggest-pain-point/1100-6532298/ (Jun 10, 2025).
- Scoring currency: studs buy Golden Bricks from Mr. Gold; "the player with the most Golden Bricks by the end of the game is the winner" — SuperPhillip Central (Jan 1, 2026).
- Its Boo equivalent is Mr. Thief: "players can choose to steal another player's studs for free, or opt to steal a Gold Brick from a player for 75 studs" — SuperPhillip Central (Jan 1, 2026). There are also Thief spaces; the "Notorious Brick Thief" trophy is "Get on a 'Thief' space in any Zone and steal a Gold Brick from another Player" — https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3594996596 (player guide, undated on page).
- Stealing a brick can be **declined by the thief**: "We did over 100 playtests, and we had some tears… The option to steal gold bricks can now be declined by the pilfering player," explicitly so parents can show mercy — GameSpot via Wayback (Jun 10, 2025).
- Bonus awards were deliberately cut at launch: SMG project director Mark Fennell on bonus stars — "I just don't like them… randomness added where it doesn't belong" — same GameSpot piece (Jun 10, 2025).
- They were then added back as an option in update 1.4.0 (Mar 19, 2026): "Players may choose to add Bonus Bricks to the end of Challenge Zones… from 1-10 bricks… Get whisked away to the Bonus Brick Zone and vote on who gets some additional lucrative Golden Bricks" — https://support.fictions.com/hc/en-us/articles/50051405107859--LEGO-Party-Update-1-4-0-Patch-Notes (Mar 19, 2026).
- Its "building" is board-building, not personal property: on a Build Space "the choices you make can change the board for everyone—sometimes adding helpful shortcuts, new challenges, or bonus rewards" — https://games-netflix.helpshift.com/hc/en/44-lego-party/faq/1688-build-spaces-customizing-the-game-board/?p=all (official help page, "Last Updated: 360d", read Nov 8, 2026). IGN describes a Theme Park build choice whose Royal Ramparts adds "a catapult for lobbing you at another random player and stealing one of their gold bricks" — IGN (Sep 30, 2025).
- Official framing of the chaos: "compete… to get as many Golden Bricks as you can, by any means necessary! But beware, watch out for monsters, traps and flying roast turkeys" — Nintendo store page (read Nov 8, 2026).
- Family affordances Mario Party lacks: no 3-vs-1 minigames ("always evenly split — either every man for himself, or… teams of two"), only nine 2v2 "Brick Battles" at launch, and a handicap option that loads players up with Gold Bricks — IGN (Sep 30, 2025); handicap and the ability to ban minigame categories are confirmed at https://jaysbrickblog.com/reviews/lego-party-review-ps5/ (Sep 30, 2025) and https://www.superphillipcentral.com/2026/01/lego-party-multi-review.html (Jan 1, 2026).
- 2v2 versions of minigames were added later, in 1.4.0 — Fictions patch notes (Mar 19, 2026).

## 3. What players and reviewers like and dislike

- Nintendo Life (9/10 tone): "the best Mario Party to date", praises boards, minigames, Pro Rules ("harder to hold onto your stars and coins, there's more opportunity to ruin things for other players") and family handling — https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/super-mario-party-jamboree (Oct 15, 2024).
- IGN (positive) defends the swinginess: "You'll probably lose some games you deserved to win, and you'll definitely steal some victories you didn't really earn, but that's all part of the fun" — https://www.ign.com/articles/super-mario-party-jamboree-review (Oct 15, 2024).
- IGN also names the sting: losing a buddy to a player who simply passes you is "frustrating to play such a long minigame that could end up being for nothing" — same review (Oct 15, 2024).
- Eurogamer (negative) says the game "fails to find the fun at almost every step", that "there simply aren't enough [stars] in play to make it feel like a real competition", that minigames pay only coins so skill is poorly rewarded, and that its AI "seemingly always manag[es] to bag the 'bonus' and 'hidden' stars" — https://www.eurogamer.net/super-mario-party-jamboree-review (Oct 15, 2024). (No score was visible in the page text.)
- IGN on LEGO Party: stealing is the comedy engine — commentary is drowned out "by fits of uncontrollable laughter or salty bickering as a hard-earned gold brick is ruthlessly snatched away… it tends to sting harder than suddenly finding a lost LEGO brick with the fleshy part of your bare foot" — https://www.ign.com/articles/lego-party-review (Sep 30, 2025).
- Polygon's family test: "after testing the game out with a 7-year-old accomplice… kids and grown-ups alike will find an even playing field in Easy mode"; it praises that "you can actually lose based on sucking — not just a fluke from surprise stars dished out at the end like in Mario Party", that "there are an abundance of ways to earn gold bricks… so you never feel stranded", and that many minigames have multiple rounds so "you get to screw up once and still win" — https://www.polygon.com/lego-party-review/ (Sep 30, 2025).
- GameSpot frames the whole genre's sore spot as late-game swings that "render strategy meaningless", and reports LEGO Party's fix came from children crying in playtests — GameSpot via Wayback (Jun 10, 2025).
- Forbes: "very well balanced for younger or more inexperienced players, allowing you to give them a head start if necessary", but some minigames are "unbalanced (or simply unfair)" (a maze game won by inaction; a manual-reading game that gives a cursor-position head start) — https://web.archive.org/web/20260102103538/https://www.forbes.com/sites/mattgardner1/2025/09/30/lego-party-review-switch-great-ideas-dodgy-execution/ (Sep 30, 2025).
- Common Sense Media rates Jamboree age 6+, "Fun party game encourages friendly social play", positive messages "social interaction, friendly competitive play, and teamwork" — https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/super-mario-party-jamboree (page undated; read Nov 8, 2026).
- Player voices: a Nintendo Life commenter on Jamboree — "My kid is 6, and we play all kinds of games together, but the problem is that he cries when he loses" — https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/super-mario-party-jamboree (comment thread, Oct 2024 onward).
- Which take-aways are remembered fondly: Chance Time is quotable and beloved enough to be merchandised by Nintendo itself — "Amazing comebacks are always possible with Chance Time!" (Tumble, Mario Party 3) — https://www.mariowiki.com/Chance_Time (wiki); and the blue shell is simultaneously "one of the best and most famous, yet also one of the most frustrating items in video game history" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell (wiki; cites Kotaku, Aug 28, 2014).
- Reddit threads on Chance Time exist ("What is your opinion about chance time spaces?") but served a login wall, so I could not quote players — https://old.reddit.com/r/MARIOPARTY/comments/qavgl7/what_is_your_opinion_about_chance_time_spaces/ (blocked).

## 4. Undoing work a player already finished

- Neither Mario Party nor LEGO Party has "board state" you can knock down: their valuables are tokens (coins/stars, studs/bricks) held by a player. LEGO Party's nearest thing, Build Spaces, change the board for everyone and are not owned or knockable — official help page, https://games-netflix.helpshift.com/hc/en/44-lego-party/faq/1688-build-spaces-customizing-the-game-board/?p=all (read Nov 8, 2026). I found no report of a LEGO Party event removing a build (see Not confirmed).
- Precedent for taking back a *token* (the closest analogue to a placed piece) is Boo / Mr. Thief / Chance Time, and every recent version of it adds friction: player-initiated (walk to Boo), costed (50 coins for a star; 75 studs for a brick), declinable (LEGO Party), or random-but-rare (1–3 Chance Time spaces per board) — https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo, https://www.mariowiki.com/Chance_Time_Space, https://www.superphillipcentral.com/2026/01/lego-party-multi-review.html, GameSpot via Wayback.
- Victim counterplay exists in the series' history: mash A to fight off Boo (Mario Party 2) — https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo (wiki).
- In Mario Kart, the blue shell was unstoppable in Mario Kart 64; "this was changed in Mario Kart: Super Circuit (2001), and subsequent games allowed ways for the targeted racer to do so… most commonly with a Mushroom", and Mario Kart 8's Super Horn can destroy it — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell (wiki).
- In Tetris-style versus play the loss is telegraphed and cancellable: incoming garbage sits in a receiving queue and "an opponent can also counter-attack by performing line clears to reduce or eliminate the incoming garbage… any leftover counter-attack amount is then sent back" — https://tetris.wiki/Garbage (wiki, read Nov 8, 2026).
- Loss aversion is the reason designers are careful here: "we feel more pain from a loss than we do pleasure from an equivalent gain. Losses are worse than gains are good. Most of the time game designers want to avoid triggering this feeling" — https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2020/09/podcast-63-loss-aversion-and-game-design/ (Sep 2020), which discusses Geoffrey Engelstein's book *Achievement Relocked: Loss Aversion and Game Design* (MIT Press; the MIT page returned 403 for me).
- Same principle with worked examples and the original Tversky/Kahneman framing experiments: "losses loom larger than gains" — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-zombies-barbarians-and-loss-aversion (undated on the re-published page; Gamasutra-era).
- Design patterns that make take-aways land as fun rather than unfair, as seen above: (a) consent — LEGO Party's declinable steal, born of "some tears" in 100+ playtests (GameSpot via Wayback); (b) cost — 75 studs / 50 coins; (c) rarity — 1–3 Chance Time spaces, 1–5 Bowser spaces; (d) advance warning and a stop — garbage queue + line-clear offset, blue-shell siren + Mushroom/Super Horn; (e) fast recovery — Polygon's "abundance of ways to earn gold bricks… you never feel stranded" (Sep 30, 2025); (f) predictability over randomness — Jamboree Pro Rules' fixed Bowser punishment, no Chance Time (mariowiki) and single announced bonus star (mariowiki Bonus Star).

## 5. Catch-up by take-away vs. slowing people down

- The blue shell is explicitly a catch-up tool: "racers ranked towards the bottom having a better chance of it spawning… giving them a better chance at diminishing the lead of the racer in top position" (citing *Characteristics of Games*, MIT Press, 2012) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell (wiki).
- Stated design intent: Hideki Konno said the shell was added "to keep players together" and "we wanted to create a race where everyone was in it until the end"; Kosuke Yabuki said Nintendo has tried removing it but "it feels like something's missing" — both quoted on the same page (Kotaku 2011; Eurogamer Jul 21, 2017).
- The objection to take-away catch-up, put sharply: "The Blue Shell steals progress from a rightfully earned win on behalf of the lazy and the incompetent" — Ian Bogost, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-blue-shell-and-its-discontents (May 30, 2014). Bogost also notes MK64's version could be dodged on its way to the leader, which is what kept it from feeling purely arbitrary.
- What the blue shell actually removes is *time and position* (a spin-out), not banked winnings — nobody loses a trophy. That distinction is visible in Mario Party too: Bowser Shuffle moves players around the board (recoverable position) while "Lose 1 Star" removes a banked token — https://www.mariowiki.com/Bowser_Space (wiki).
- Nintendo's own "fairness valve" for targeted punishment is predictability plus a floor for the loser: Jamboree Pro Rules make Bowser's star theft non-random, and last place may instead receive 20 coins — same Bowser Space page (wiki).
- LEGO Party's chosen catch-up is skill-based order rather than theft: minigame placement sets next round's turn order, so the round winner "could beat you to the gold brick" — GameSpot via Wayback (Jun 10, 2025); SuperPhillip notes players may even "throw it completely" to get a better order (Jan 1, 2026). Its theft is gated by a 75-stud price — SuperPhillip Central (Jan 1, 2026).
- Mariowiki's characterisation of Chance Time as a "very risky gamble" whose value is "Amazing comebacks are always possible" shows the series selling the drama, not the fairness — https://www.mariowiki.com/Chance_Time (wiki).
- Overall: targeting the leader reads as fair when it (i) hits position/tempo, (ii) is telegraphed, (iii) can be blocked, and (iv) does not delete banked earnings; it reads as unfair when it deletes the thing the player earned, unseen, at the end (Eurogamer's "there simply aren't enough [stars] in play"; GameSpot's "render strategy meaningless").

## 6. Scoring, bonuses, ties, scoreboards

- Per-minigame pay: Mario Party minigames pay **coins by placement** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Party (2018); LEGO Party pays studs, which buy Golden Bricks — https://www.superphillipcentral.com/2026/01/lego-party-multi-review.html (Jan 1, 2026). Only stars/bricks decide the win.
- Star prices vary by game and by board event: 20 coins (Superstars, Jamboree), 10 coins (Super Mario Party), 10 coins for one or 20 for two in Rainbow Galleria flash sales, 40 coins under a Markup Sticker, and two-for-20-coins-each with Peach as a Jamboree Buddy — https://www.mariowiki.com/Star_(Mario_Party_series) (wiki).
- End-of-game bonuses, Mario Party: "Most Mario Party games have three Bonus Stars", but Super Mario Party, Superstars and Jamboree give **two**, and three only if the game lasts 20 turns (SMP) or 30 turns (Superstars/Jamboree); Superstars and Jamboree also offer "only the three classic Bonus Stars (Rich, Minigame, Eventful)"; Jamboree's Pro Rules are "always 12 turns with one Bonus Star… announced at the beginning of the game" — https://www.mariowiki.com/Bonus_Star (wiki).
- Bonus Star categories are things like Minigame Star (most minigame coins or wins), Rich Star (most coins collected), Item Star, Sightseer Star (most spaces moved), Slowpoke Star (lowest dice total), Misfortune Star (most red/unlucky spaces), Bowser Space Star — same Bonus Star page (wiki). IGN confirms the Pro Rules variant: "the singular Bonus Star category is revealed before the match starts so you can tailor your moves" (Oct 15, 2024).
- LEGO Party at launch: no bonus bricks at all (GameSpot via Wayback; IGN: "LEGO Party doesn't bother with random participation awards at the end of each board… the winner is always clearly defined by the time you reach the end", Sep 30, 2025). Since 1.4.0 they exist but are **opt-in, 1–10 of them, and voted on with the losers in a "Bonus Brick Zone"** — Fictions patch notes (Mar 19, 2026).
- Double-value effects: Jamboree Buddies double every action (two stars, Boo twice, Bowser twice) — IGN (Oct 15, 2024); Sky/Peach buddy two-for-one bricks — mariowiki Star page.
- Ties: Super Mario Party defines the winner as whoever has "the most stars and coins", i.e. coins as the tiebreak — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Party (2018). I could not confirm the tie rule for Superstars/Jamboree or LEGO Party.
- Scoreboard between rounds: LEGO Party keeps a running stud/brick display and ends on an interactive results screen where players can shove each other — IGN (Sep 30, 2025); Jamboree stores per-mode game data in its Party Plaza hub — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Party_Jamboree (wiki). I found no source describing a between-round scoreboard animation in either game.

## Summary for our cat (15 lines)

1. Verdict: the cat should **not** knock away a *locked* piece by default. Knocking away a piece that is still loose (in hand, in flight, in the pile) is good, cheap chaos and should stay.
2. Why: a locked piece is our Gold Brick, and LEGO Party's own designers found that taking exactly that token made children cry in 100+ playtests (GameSpot, Jun 10, 2025).
3. Their fix was not "less stealing" — it was making the theft *declinable by the thief* so an adult can spare a child. Our equivalent: let the cat be shooed, and let the shoo always work.
4. Loss aversion means the sting of losing one placed piece is bigger than the joy of placing one; that is a bad trade in a 3–5 round night (Madigan, Sep 2020).
5. Mario Party's own escape hatch is Pro Rules: no Chance Time, fixed Bowser punishment, one announced bonus star. Note that the "hardcore" mode is the one that removes surprise losses.
6. If you do allow it, put it behind a rules toggle ("Mischief"), default off, and say so on the party-setup screen.
7. Limit: at most one bat-away per round, and never in the final round or in the last 10 seconds of a picture.
8. Limit: the cat may only reach the *leading* side's board (blue-shell targeting reads as fairer than random), and only if that side is ahead by a piece.
9. Warning: 2 seconds is fine, but make it loud and specific — the cat's shadow and a squawk on the exact piece it is about to hit (Mario Party shows the space, then a roulette; Tetris shows the incoming garbage).
10. Stop it: "shoo" must be a real counter — a thumb tap that always works within the warning, with the shooed cat sulking off for the rest of the round.
11. Give back fast: the knocked piece should not be destroyed. Have it bounce to that side's spawn point and glow in its outline, so it is one carry away — "you never feel stranded" (Polygon, Sep 30, 2025).
12. Never take the *last* piece of a picture, and never take a piece the same side placed in the last ~3 seconds (that reads as erasing, not as mischief).
13. Score the cat's damage as noise, not as a point swing: it costs a few seconds of rework, not score. Scoreboard should show pieces placed, not pieces lost.
14. If playtests show 8-year-olds laughing rather than crying, you can graduate it to one locked-piece bat-away per picture in "Mischief" mode only. Test with the youngest player winning and losing.
15. Our own tests to run: does a shooed cat feel like a win? Does a rebuilt piece feel faster than the first placement? If either answer is no, keep the cat off the locked pieces.

## Questions for you (Claude)

1. The brief says LEGO Party is published by Fireshine Games. Every primary source (LEGO.com press release Jun 6, 2025, Nintendo store, Wikipedia) says **Fictions**, the label that absorbed Private Division. Should the brief be corrected?
2. Do you want the cat to be a *time* cost (piece returns to spawn) or a *progress* cost (piece removed from the picture and its outline cleared)? All the evidence supports time-only.
3. Is "leader-only targeting" acceptable for a 2-player race (one side is always the leader)? If not, the toggle is the only safeguard.
4. Should the shoo be per-player (a thumb tap) or per-side (any player on that side)? Per-side is more forgiving for an 8-year-old who is mid-carry.
5. Do you want me to look for a specific commercial family game that already does "knock the built tower down" (Jenga/Rhino Hero family) as a design precedent? I stopped short of stacking-game sources to stay inside the brief.
