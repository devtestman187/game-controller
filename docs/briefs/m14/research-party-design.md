Context: Thumb Party is a family party game played on a TV, with phones as controllers, for groups that include children around 8. Its main game, Shape Race, is a cooperative-competitive puzzle: 1 to 4 players on two sides race to carry, turn and drop shapes into a picture's outlines. The next milestone adds party features: chaos events during a round (a cat that walks in and bats a loose piece away, after a warning; players can shoo it), power-ups from a star, points for actions, a "party night" of 3 or 5 rounds with a scoreboard, and end-of-night awards with bonus points. Our own characters and art only; no online features.

Questions (answer each with the URL of the page that says so, and its date):
1. Catch-up ("rubber band") mechanics in family party and racing games: how they're done, what players, parents and reviewers call fair or unfair, and what to avoid (players feeling robbed).
2. Random events and chaos in party games: warning before the event, how often, how long, and how designers keep it fun for children rather than frustrating. Any guidance on a chaos or difficulty setting.
3. End-of-night or bonus awards (bonus stars and similar): how awards are chosen, how many, how they're revealed, and how players feel about awards deciding the winner.
4. Points and scoreboards for mixed-age groups: showing scores between rounds, last round worth double, and keeping losing players engaged.
5. Designing for children around 8: reading level, time pressure, losing gracefully, cooperative versus competitive play, from game design writing, UX research or child development sources.
6. Accessibility and comfort: flashing or dark screens ("lights out"), motion, colour-blind play, sound cues.

Prefer design talks and articles (GDC Vault summaries, Game Developer (gamedeveloper.com), designers' blogs), reviews that discuss fairness, and child UX research (Nielsen Norman Group, academic papers). Describe other games' mechanics in your own words; quote no more than a short phrase.

In the report: one section per question, facts as short bullets each with its URL; a "Not confirmed" list; and a 15-line summary of design rules for our party features. Keep it under 220 lines.
