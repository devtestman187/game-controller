Step 2a gave toy_look the values (ToyColors, ToySizes, ToyType, ToyLook, ToyLookScope, toyTheme). This job adds the four widgets every menu will be built from. No app adopts them yet except the Look test screen's gallery: menus move in step 3.

Claude's rules:
1. All four widgets read the look with `ToyLookScope.of(context)`; nothing is drawn from a literal colour or size (review.sh's `color` check). Shadows are the hard sticker kind: the shape's outline filled with `colors.ink` at `toyShadowAlpha`, moved by `sizes.shadowOffset`, no blur.
2. **ToyButton** (design section 4), `ToyButton({required String label, required VoidCallback? onPressed, Color? color, IconData? icon, FocusNode? focusNode, bool autofocus = false, bool showFocus = true})`:
   - A pill: fill `color ?? colors.good`, ink outline `sizes.outlineWidth`, the hard shadow under it; the label in `type.button` and ink (D12), an optional icon before it in ink.
   - Height at least `sizes.buttonHeight`: add it to ToySizes, 48 on menus (the touch-target minimum) and 64 on TV; horizontal padding 24 (`space24`).
   - Pressed (pointer down, or the activate key held): the shadow is gone and the pill sits `shadowOffset` lower and to the right, so it looks pushed in.
   - Focused, and `showFocus` true: a `sizes.focusRing` ring in `colors.focus` outside the ink edge with its own thin ink edge (1.5), and a bounce 1.0 → 1.06 → 1.04 over 220 ms when the focus arrives; with `look.effects` false there's no bounce, it just sits at 1.04. `showFocus` false (a touch-first host, M12 bug 1107f: the focus look doesn't show there) draws no ring and no scale.
   - Disabled (`onPressed` null): paper fill, a dashed ink edge (dashes 6 on, 4 off), no shadow, not focusable, taps ignored.
   - Activated by a tap, and with the focus on it by Enter, Space, Select (the TV remote's centre) and the gamepad A button: use the framework's ActivateIntent (FocusableActionDetector) so the TV's D-pad navigation works as it does for today's buttons.
   - Semantics: a button with the label.
3. **ToyCard** (design section 4), `ToyCard({required Widget child, String? header, Color? headerColor, EdgeInsets padding = const EdgeInsets.all(16), bool popIn = true})`:
   - Paper, ink edge `sizes.outlineWidth`, the hard shadow, `sizes.cardCorner` corners, `clipBehavior` so the header band follows the corners.
   - An optional header band across the top in `headerColor ?? colors.focus` with `header` in `type.button`, ink, and an ink line under the band.
   - What it holds gets ink text (Claude's 2a review: text follows the screen, and paper is a screen of its own): a `Theme` whose textTheme is the outer one with `bodyColor` and `displayColor` ink and whose listTileTheme text and icon colours are ink, and a `DefaultTextStyle.merge` in ink with `type.body`.
   - Pop-in: scale 0.9 → 1.0 with a small overshoot (`Curves.easeOutBack`) over 180 ms when it first appears, if `popIn` and `look.effects`; otherwise it appears at 1.0.
4. **ToyBadge**: a pure function `void paintToyBadge(Canvas canvas, Offset centre, double diameter, int number, ToyLook look)` (painters use it for the players' dots, step 4) and a widget `ToyBadge({required int number, double diameter = 40})` that paints with it. A circle in `colors.player(number)`, ink edge (`diameter / 14`, at least 2), the hard shadow scaled with the diameter (`shadowOffset * diameter / 40`), and the number centred in Fredoka (`type.title`'s family and weight) at `diameter * 0.55`, ink (D10, D12: the number is always there, so players aren't told apart by colour alone). The widget's semantics label is "Player n".
5. **ToyBackground**, `ToyBackground({Widget? child})`: fills its box with `colors.background`, a radial glow from `colors.backgroundGlow` at the centre to `background` at 0.75 of the longer side, and faint polka dots on a staggered grid (spacing 32, radius 3, every other row shifted by 16) in white at 22% (Sunny) or 7% (Night): put those two alphas in ToyColors as `dotAlpha` so the palette owns them. Still, no motion (the drifting dots are the shader's, step 6). The painting is a `CustomPainter` whose `shouldRepaint` is true only when the look changes, inside a `RepaintBoundary`, with the child on top.
6. **The gallery** on the TV app's Look test screen: a second section (a toggle or a tab beside the shader rows, keep the shader rows as they are) showing the four widgets under a ToyLookScope and `toyTheme` it builds itself, with a Sunny/Night switch: ToyBackground filling the section, buttons in four states (normal, focused, disabled, and a second colour), a ToyCard with a header holding a line of body text and a ListTile, badges 1 to 6, and one line per ToyType style. It's how the owner sees the widgets on a TV and a PC before step 3. Use `ToyLook.build(palette: …, tv: true)`. The screen file stays under 400 lines: put the gallery in its own file.
7. Export the new widgets from toy_look.dart. Change nothing else.
