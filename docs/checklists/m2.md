# Milestone 2 checklist: D-pad mode and input haptics

Copied from the PRD acceptance tests. Run it with the `m2` builds: the iPhone `.ipa` from Codemagic (tag `m2`), the TV APK, and the Windows TV app if you like. Android phone items are marked *(deferred)*.

Setup: install both builds, open **Controller Test** on the TV, open **Controller** on the iPhone. It reconnects to the TV from M1 by itself (or shows the Connect screen). The controller screen has the D-pad on the left, Select/Start in the middle and A/B/X/Y on the right. The TV shows each controller with a small picture of its buttons.

## Acceptance tests
- [X] **Every press and release shows on the TV and ticks on the phone (iPhone).** Press each of A, B, X, Y, Start, Select and the D-pad directions, including diagonals. *(Expect: the TV's picture lights the same control while held and turns it off on release; the phone ticks on press and, lighter, on release. The text under Start on the phone describes what's held.)*
- [ ] *(deferred)* The same on the Android phone.
- [X] **Direction plus buttons at once.** Hold right on the D-pad and press A, then A and B together. Slide the D-pad thumb around without lifting. *(Expect: the TV shows the direction and both buttons together; each new direction ticks.)*
- [X] **Intensity and off.** Haptics icon (vibration symbol) → move the intensity slider and press **Test**, then turn **Tick on every press** off. *(Expect: ticks get stronger and weaker; when off, no ticks but input still reaches the TV. The setting is kept after relaunching the app.)* Android phone: *(deferred)*.
- [X] **No stuck input.** Hold a button and the D-pad, then (a) swipe up to go home, (b) pull down Control Center, (c) turn Wi-Fi off. Also try **Drop connections** on the TV while holding. *(Expect: in every case the TV shows everything released, nothing stays lit. After returning or reconnecting, holding a button again shows it.)*
- [X] **Haptics support in Diagnostics.** Diagnostics → **Haptics** shows Supported: Yes, Engine: Core Haptics, Intensity control: Yes, and the ticks played count rising. With haptics turned off the app runs normally. *(A device without haptics would show Supported: No. The iPhone has them, so this is covered by the automated tests.)*
- [X] **First latency impression.** Tap buttons while watching the TV and note how it feels (instant / slight delay / laggy), plus the ping line at the top of the phone (`30 s: min · avg · max`) and the avg/max on the TV. Write it in the Result section below, for comparison in M5.

## Extra checks (not in the PRD, quick)
- [ ] TV: **Simulate controller** now also presses every button and direction in a loop, and the TV picture follows it.
- [ ] Laptop: `cd packages\connection; dart run connection:sim_controller --host <TV IP> --code <code> --demo` does the same from the PC.
- [ ] Sliding a thumb from A to B releases A and presses B. Sliding off a button releases it.
- [ ] The Gyro mode button is shown but greyed out (it arrives in M3).
- [ ] Diagnostics → Share includes the haptics line and the 30 s ping summary.

## Already verified on the PC (2026-10-24)
- Automated: 31 protocol tests, 47 networking tests (input delivery, order, before-pairing input ignored, a held button resent after a reconnect, release on disconnect, 30 s ping summary), 40 phone tests (D-pad sectors, face button hit areas, multi-touch, sliding, ticks per change, haptics off/unsupported/failing, release when leaving the app), 5 TV tests.
- The Windows TV app with the command-line simulator in `--demo` mode: the TV picture showed down-left on the D-pad, then B, with a 2–3 ms ping.

## What can't be tested on emulators or Windows
| Area | Why | Where tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core Haptics feel and intensity | Needs a Taptic Engine | iPhone |
| VibrationEffect feel | Needs a real motor | Android phone (deferred) |
| Multi-touch with thumbs | Real touch screen | iPhone |
| Backgrounding and Control Center | Real iOS lifecycle | iPhone |

## Result
Passed on the iPhone and the TV (2026-10-24, Android phone deferred). User: "haptics and responses feel solid on iOS", and everything looked right on the TV. No separate latency numbers were recorded, so M5 starts from the phone and TV ping summaries.
