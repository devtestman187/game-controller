Privacy policy

For the Thumb Party app (phones and tablets) and the Thumb Party TV app (Android TV and Windows). Effective November 1, 2026 (version 0.9.0). The previous version, from October 29, 2026, said no data was collected.

In short: the apps don't collect, sell or share any personal information. They have no accounts, no ads and no advertising or analytics tools. From version 0.9.0 the TV app can send anonymous gameplay stats to the developer, but only if an adult says yes on the TV. Without that, everything stays on your devices and your home Wi-Fi.

What the apps do with data

  • Controller input stays on your Wi-Fi. The phone sends button presses, stick positions and tilt directly to the TV app on the same local network. Nothing goes over the internet.
  • Pairing and settings stay on the device. To reconnect without the code, the phone and the TV app each save a random pairing code and a random device ID. Your settings (vibration strength, layout, and so on) are saved on the device too. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Device names. The TV app shows each phone as something like "iPhone 3F1C" and the phone shows the TV's name, only on the local network.
  • Diagnostics. The phone's Diagnostics screen shows connection details, battery and temperature readings. They aren't sent anywhere. If you tap Share, the report goes only to the app and person you choose.

Anonymous gameplay stats

To find out which controls feel good and which are hard to use, the apps count how the controls are used. This is how it works:

  • The phone counts, the TV collects. While a phone is connected to the TV app, it counts things like how often each button is pressed and for how long, taps that miss a control (counted on a coarse grid, never a trace of your touches), how the sticks and tilt are used, and the settings in use. It sends these counts to the TV app on your Wi-Fi only. The TV app adds how quick the connection is, how the games went, and the answer to "How did the controls feel?" after some games.
  • What it includes about the device: phone or tablet, screen size, model (for example "Google Pixel 8"), operating system version, whether it can vibrate, and a random ID the app makes for each install. Not the device's name, the advertising ID or any hardware ID.
  • What it never includes: names or nicknames, accounts, email addresses, contacts, photos, location, Wi-Fi or network details, or advertising IDs.
  • Kept on the TV either way. The TV app keeps a summary of each play session on the device, which you can see on its Stats screen and export to a file.
  • Sent only with an adult's yes. The first time the TV app starts, it asks the adult in charge whether to share the stats. Only then does it send each session summary, over an encrypted connection, to a database the developer runs on Google Firebase (Firestore). The answer can be changed at any time on the Stats screen. Nothing is shared by default.
  • Each phone can opt out: in the phone app, Settings → Control stats shows that stats are recorded and turns them off for that phone.
  • How it's used: only to make the controls and games better. It's never sold, never used for ads, and never shared with anyone else. Firebase stores it for the developer as a service provider.
  • Deleting it: the stats aren't linked to a person. To have sessions deleted, email the developer the Controller ID shown on the phone's Diagnostics screen. Uninstalling the TV app deletes the sessions kept on the TV.

Permissions

  • Local network: to find and connect to the TV app on your Wi-Fi.
  • Vibration: for the buzz when you press a button or something happens in the game.
  • Motion sensors: for gyro mode, where tilting the phone steers. The readings are turned into controller input on the phone and sent only to the TV app.

Children

Thumb Party is made for families to play together, including children aged 6 and up. The apps collect no personal information from anyone, so they collect none from children: no names, contact details, photos, location or advertising IDs, no accounts, and no ads. The anonymous gameplay stats above are shared only if an adult agrees on the TV. The apps meet Google Play's Families policy.

Changes

Any change to this policy will be posted on this page with a new date, before the apps change. If the stats ever include anything new, the TV app will ask again.
